DARK, AUTHENTIC CASTLEVANIA DETAIL
Aim for serious, sombre gothic castle game art: old, heavy, believable and atmospheric, with material colours guided by each source. Retain source-red/brown stone as visibly red-brown; keep greys, wood, fog, vegetation and other materials in their own source colour families. Create darkness through local shadow depth, recessed joints, selective directional lighting and believable weathering. Do not wash the art into grey-brown monochrome, apply a global cold/desaturation filter, or push it to bright candy orange. Keep details legible at game scale.

Add more authentic detail than the sparse source pixels can show, but put detail where the material and structure justify it: uneven hand-worked stone faces, subtle mineral variation, worn corners, a few structurally plausible chips or cracks, aged mortar, damp streaks only below or near plausible water paths, soot in sheltered recesses, restrained moss in joints and ledges. Vary the stones naturally instead of stamping the same texture on every face. Allow areas of calmer stone so the architecture remains readable.

Avoid toy-like rounded cushion stones, inflated identical bevels, cartoon outlines, glossy/plastic surfaces, bright uniform edge highlights, and flat clean masonry. Also avoid all-over grain, glitter, metallic flecks, random speckles, noisy sharpening and repeated procedural crack patterns. More detail means specific, restrained material storytelling, not noise everywhere. Keep vegetation at the amount and placement supported by the source; do not invent ivy curtains or decorative clutter.
